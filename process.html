Process

A result of deep seeing.

Her phrase, from her card for Whispers on Bricks, 2025

A drawing of hers begins well before the pen, with a stretch of pavement, a book and sometimes a bowl of pulp. More than once it has ended back on the floor, among breeze blocks and branches. These are the steps in between.

i

i. Choosing a site

Moss asks for almost nothing. A crack, a little rain, to be left alone.

What held her were the patches nobody tends: moss filling the joints of brick paving, a green tuft on the top of a brick, a cushion of moss on old bark.

ii

ii. Down at ankle height

She photographs from above and close in, with the camera pointed straight at the ground. Pavement seams. A crack in old paving gone rust-orange and green. Moss where the tarmac meets the kerb. Lichen. In one photograph, a pair of trainers stands at the edge of the frame.

iii

iii. A podcast, then a book

The book is Gathering Moss by the botanist Robin Wall Kimmerer. Passages from it, copied out by hand onto torn slips of paper, end up pinned beside her drawings and laid among the moss on the floor.

Early in 2024 she turned the reading into a storyboard and called it the close encounter.

iv

iv. Making the paper first

Early in 2024 she made paper with moss in the pulp. The steps: moss in a clear bag, moss laid out on cloth, a bowl of brown water, pulp, a wet sheet thick with green, and a round sheet drying. Then she drew on the round sheets in ink, so a drawing of moss sits on paper made partly of moss.

v

v. Then the pen

In 2023 she made a fourteen-second reel of how she draws, with one word over each shot. A card drawing is held up to a park. A sheet is taped to a board. Pencil comes first. A narrow trial strip holds a single inked oval, a planting plan sits over grid paper, and a house takes on colour, with the pens lying beside the page.

One sheet, closer and closer

One drawing of a moss clump, 2024, seen whole and then closer and closer. Read inwards, it shows what the pen does.

  1. 1The whole clump, on bare paper.

  2. 2Closer. The dense middle, with grey shadow around it.

  3. 3Closer again. Here the leaves are drawn one by one in pen, and around them is a softer grey.

  4. 4Each leaf is a small star of strokes.

  5. 5The edge, where the drawing thins into the paper.

vi

vi. Laid on the floor

In 2024 the drawings came down off the wall. She laid out breeze blocks planted with living moss, wooden pallets, branches, fallen leaves, photographs of paving and sheets of moss paper, with the drawings among them, all on the floor. She called it “monumentalising moss by staging”. Visitors crouched to look. That September, in a white room, the drawings lay on low plinths of stacked blocks under the label Encounter moss / From the soil.

vii

vii. Something to hold

In 2025 the moss went into a box. CITY MOSSES, says the lid. Inside are loose drawings, cards, and leaflets titled Watch Your Step! an ask of the city mosses. Visitors opened it at a table topped with handmade paper and went through it sheet by sheet. A book of the drawings, bound with screw posts, went out into the long grass to be photographed.

viii

viii. Watch your step.

That is the title she gave the box, and the request the city mosses make of anyone passing over them. Look down as you go.
